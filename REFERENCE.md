# dieselsim — complete reference

Every configurable variable, every output, every term of art, and how each one
actually gets used. Values quoted are the `hd_i6` defaults unless stated.

**Units are SI throughout** unless a field name says otherwise (`_mg`, `_deg`,
`_kmh`, `_h`, `_rpm`). Pressures are absolute Pa unless the name says `gauge`.

---

## 0. The crank-angle convention

Everything in the package uses one convention, and getting it wrong is the
single easiest way to produce nonsense:

```
theta = 0 deg    TDC firing (combustion TDC)
0   -> 180       expansion
180 -> 360       exhaust stroke
360 -> 540       intake stroke
540 -> 720       compression stroke
```

So `soi_deg_btdc = 8.0` means injection starts at `theta = 712`, and
`evo_deg = 130` means the exhaust valve cracks 50° before BDC on the power
stroke.

`Geometry.phase_deg(i)` returns where cylinder `i` sits relative to cylinder 1,
derived from `firing_order`. For an I6 firing 1-5-3-6-2-4, cylinder 5 is at
120°, cylinder 3 at 240°, and so on.

## 1. Terminology

You need these to read anything else.

### Mean effective pressures

An MEP is work per cycle divided by displacement — it converts an engine of any
size into a pressure, so a 1.5 L and a 12.7 L become directly comparable.

| Term | Meaning | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **IMEP gross** | Indicated work over the closed part of the cycle (compression + expansion only) | integral of `p dV` from IVC to EVO |
| **IMEP net** | Gross minus the pumping loop | `imep_gross + pmep` |
| **PMEP** | Pumping work — the cost of breathing. Negative when the engine spends work to breathe; can go **positive** on a well-matched turbo when intake pressure exceeds exhaust | integral of `p dV` over the gas-exchange strokes |
| **FMEP** | Friction work, all mechanical losses plus accessories | `friction.evaluate()` — computed, never fitted |
| **BMEP** | What reaches the flywheel | `imep_net − fmep` |

Rules of thumb: 20–24 bar BMEP is a modern heavy-duty diesel at full load,
17–21 bar a small car diesel, 8–11 bar naturally aspirated.

### Efficiencies

- **η_mech** = BMEP / IMEP_net. How much indicated work survives friction.
  88–95% typical; falls at low load because friction is roughly constant.
- **η_brake** = brake power / fuel power. 40–46% for a good diesel at its sweet
  spot.
- **BSFC** (g/kWh) = fuel mass flow / brake power. The reciprocal of η_brake in
  practical clothing. 190–215 g/kWh is a good HD diesel; below ~185 is suspect.
- **ISFC** — same but against indicated power, so it excludes friction.

### Combustion

- **Ignition delay** — crank angle (or ms) between start of injection and start
  of combustion. Fuel is being sprayed but not burning; it accumulates, and
  when it lights, it lights all at once.
- **Premixed fraction** — how much of the fuel burned in that first bang. High
  premix means a steep `dp/dθ`, which is diesel knock. Both the noise and the
  NOx come from here.
- **Diffusion burn** — the rest, burning as fast as it can mix. Rate-limited by
  the injector, not by chemistry, which is why `rail_pressure` and `n_holes`
  change the burn shape.
- **CA50 / `mfb50`** — crank angle at which 50% of the fuel has burned. The
  single best summary of combustion phasing. 8–15° ATDC is normal; earlier
  raises peak pressure and NOx, later costs efficiency.
- **Wiebe function** — the S-curve used to describe burn fraction vs angle.
  Three superposed ones here: pilot, premixed main, diffusion main.
- **AFR / φ** — air-fuel ratio, and equivalence ratio `φ = AFR_stoich / AFR`.
  Diesels run lean overall (AFR 18–60); φ is what actually matters locally.
- **Smoke limit** — the minimum AFR you can run before soot becomes visible.
  `spec.afr_limit`. It's what caps fuelling at low boost, and it's why a diesel
  bogs before the turbo spools.

### Tribology

- **Lambda ratio (λ)** — film thickness divided by combined surface roughness.
  The single most important number in the lubrication model.
  - λ < 1: boundary lubrication. Asperities touching. This is where wear
    happens.
  - 1 < λ < 3: mixed. Most of a real engine, most of the time.
  - λ > 3: full film. No wear, friction is pure viscous shear.
- **Stribeck curve** — friction vs `μN/P`. High at low speed (boundary), falls
  through mixed, rises again in full film as viscous drag takes over.
- **Boundary friction power** — the part of friction generated by asperity
  contact. In this package it is what drives wear (see §7).
- **Hydrodynamic / EHL** — pressure generated by dragging oil into a converging
  gap. EHL adds elastic deformation of the surfaces and the pressure-thickening
  of the oil, which is what lets a cam lobe survive at all.

### Turbocharging

- **PR** (pressure ratio) — compressor outlet / inlet absolute pressure. "2.5
  bar boost" in this package means PR = 2.5, i.e. ~1.5 bar gauge.
- **Corrected speed / flow** — turbo maps are quoted corrected to reference
  conditions so one map covers all inlet states. `n_corr = n / sqrt(T/T_ref)`.
- **Surge** — flow too low for the pressure being asked. The flow reverses,
  violently and repeatedly. `surge_margin < 0` means you're inside the line.
- **Choke** — maximum flow the wheel can pass; the speed line goes vertical.
- **BSR** (blade speed ratio) — turbine tip speed / ideal spouting velocity.
  Efficiency peaks near 0.68, which is why turbine sizing matters so much.
- **VGT** — variable geometry turbine. Vanes close at low flow to raise
  expansion ratio, which is how a modern diesel makes boost at 1500 rpm.
- **A/R** — the classic housing sizing parameter. Represented here by
  `turbine_area_eff`.

### Driveline

- **SR** (speed ratio) — turbine speed / pump speed in a converter.
- **TR** (torque ratio) — turbine torque / pump torque. ~2 at stall, exactly 1
  above the coupling point.
- **Stall speed** — the engine speed a converter settles at against a braked
  output at full throttle. **This is how a converter is specified**, not the
  engine's rated speed.
- **Coupling point** — the SR where the stator freewheels and TR reaches 1
  (~0.86 here).
- **Lockup** — a clutch that bypasses the fluid entirely. Converter efficiency
  is TR × SR, so lockup is worth a large fuel saving.
- **Torque phase / inertia phase** — the two halves of a clutch-to-clutch
  shift. See §9.

## 11. Module constants worth knowing

`thermo.py`
- `_CP_A_INF, _CP_A_AMP, _CP_A_TAU = 1310, 393, 1185` — air `cp` fit,
  `cp = inf − amp·exp(−T/τ)`.
- `_CP_B_INF, _CP_B_AMP, _CP_B_TAU = 1500, 480, 1100` — burned products.
- `_T_DISS = 2200`, `_K_DISS = 0.35` — above 2200 K, dissociation absorbs
  energy. Without this, peak temperatures come out hundreds of K too high and
  NOx is badly overpredicted.
- `Fuel.LHV = 42.7e6` J/kg, `Fuel.AFR_stoich = 14.5`.

`cycle.py`
- `TAU_MIX_REV = 1.25` — burned-gas entrainment timescale, in crank
  revolutions, scaled `(1800/rpm)^0.55`. Mixing is driven by injection
  turbulence as well as piston motion, so it does not scale as 1/rpm.
- `TAU_WALL = 0.11` — burned-zone wall loss timescale.
- `NOX_CAL = 0.030`, `SOOT_CAL = 0.28` — the two calibration constants in the
  package. Emissions are the one place with fitted scalars.
- `GAMMA_B = 1.27` — burned-gas ratio used in the two-zone pressure response.

`wear.py` — `K_ARCHARD` dimensionless wear coefficients:
`ring 1.95e-10`, `bore 1.14e-9`, `skirt 5.5e-10`, `main_bearing 1.1e-9`,
`rod_bearing 1.4e-9`, `cam 3.2e-9`. The ring is an order of magnitude below the
bore because it's nitrided and the bore isn't.

## 13. `play.py` — the driveline

### The switch
```python
TRANSMISSION = "dct"    # "tc" = torque-converter auto, "dct" = dual clutch
```

### `Vehicle`
`mass`, `r_wheel`, `gears` (list of ratios), `final`, `CdA` (drag area, m²),
`Crr` (rolling resistance coefficient), `eta` (driveline efficiency),
`J_trans`, `J_wheel`, `TR_stall`, `stall_rpm`, `v_lock_min`, `launch_rpm`,
`clutch_cap_max`, `trans`.

`stall_rpm` is the important one: a converter is specified by stall speed, not
by the engine's rated speed. Size it off rated and a high-revving engine gets a
converter that lets it flare instead of pull.

### `TorqueConverter`
`k_cap = T_ref/ω_stall²`. Then `T_pump = k_cap·λ(SR)·ω_e²` and
`T_turbine = TR(SR)·T_pump`. `λ` is flat to about SR 0.5 then collapses (the
real K-factor shape), reaches zero at SR = 1 — no slip, no torque — and goes
negative beyond, which is engine braking through the converter.

### `Gearbox`
`gear`, `gear_from`, `auto`, `neutral`, `phase` (IDLE/TORQUE/INERTIA),
`T_TORQUE`, `T_INERTIA`, `HOLD_S`, `up_rpm`, `dn_rpm`, `err0`, `torque_cut`.

Shifts are scheduled on **turbine** speed, not engine speed. With the converter
slipping at a standing start the engine sits on the governor regardless of
gear, so an engine-speed map upshifts straight into top. The downshift
threshold is derived from the actual ratio step, or the box hunts.

### `Driveline`
States: `v` (m/s), `w_in` (input shaft speed — a **state**, not a function of
road speed), `rigid` (DCT clamped), `i_eff` (ratio actually driven through).
Controls: `grade` (road gradient as a fraction), `brake` (0–1),
`lock_allowed`.

## 14. The feedback loops

The point of the package is that these are closed, not scripted.

**Wear → everything.** Ring gap grows → blow-by rises → trapped mass falls →
IMEP falls. Bearing clearance grows → leakage ∝ clearance³ → gallery pressure
falls → films thin → wear accelerates. Cam wears → lift lost → breathing
suffers. Injector cokes → burn lengthens → soot rises.

**Oil → friction → wear → oil.** Boundary friction generates the wear (Archard
in energy form, `V = k·E_boundary/(μ_b·H)`); wear debris and blow-by age the
oil; aged oil thickens and loses TBN; that changes viscosity and boundary
friction coefficient, which changes the wear rate.

**EGR trade-off.** Not scripted anywhere. Dilution means the flame must heat
inert products as well as air, so more charge mass is entrained per kg of fuel
and flame temperature drops — Zeldovich NOx falls hard. The same dilution
displaces oxygen, so Hiroyasu soot oxidation slows and soot rises. And the
high-pressure loop needs exhaust-to-intake ΔP, so the VGT must close, which
costs pumping work.

**Thermal.** Wall temperatures follow the coolant, so a cold engine loses more
heat per cycle, has a longer ignition delay, a bigger premixed spike and a
sharper `dp/dθ` — which is exactly why a cold diesel rattles.

## 15. Known limitations

- The burned zone is "two-zone-lite": one lumped burned region with air
  entrainment, not a multi-zone spray model. NOx and soot each carry one
  calibration constant.
- Manifolds are filling-and-emptying, not 1-D gas dynamics. Ram tuning is
  approximated and the acoustic waveguide is a separate downstream model.
- Compressor and turbine maps are similitude-generated, not measured.
- **`n_cycles=6` is not converged.** Five identical calls drift 245 → 252 N·m;
  at 14 cycles it settles near 267. Results computed at 6 cycles run a few
  percent light. Use 9+ for anything you intend to trust.
- `operating_point` warm-starts from the previous call, so results are mildly
  path-dependent. `batch.py` avoids this by giving every point its own engine.
- Fuelling is open-loop on rpm, not closed on measured boost. Improving the
  turbo therefore does **not** raise fuelling or torque on its own.
- Wear coefficients are literature values, not measurements of your engine.
- No tyre model in `play.py`: nothing stops tractive force exceeding grip.
